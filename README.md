## The core problem: repeat work, scattered information

Cleaning and fitness work is rarely a one-off job. Clients book again, change times, ask for a different service, or pause for a holiday. When bookings live in messages, client details live in a notebook, and payments live in a separate spreadsheet, three things go wrong:

1. Appointments get double-booked or forgotten.
2. Important client details are lost when a different team member takes the job.
3. Invoices are sent late, and unpaid work is hard to spot.

The fix is a simple, repeatable system: one client record, one booking calendar, and one payment record for every job or session.

## 1. Recurring bookings that actually stay organized

Recurring bookings are the backbone of both industries. A cleaning client may need a weekly visit; a fitness client may train twice a week for a set period.

### For cleaning businesses

- Set a clear pattern from the start: weekly, every two weeks, or monthly, on the same weekday and time where possible.
- Record the service type for each visit — for example, standard clean, deep clean, kitchen and bathroom focus, or office clean — so the team knows what to bring and how long to allow.
- Leave buffer time between jobs for travel, especially for teams covering several homes or offices in one day.
- Confirm the next visit before leaving the current one, and send a reminder the day before.
- Have a simple rule for changes: clients confirm a reschedule at least 24 hours ahead, and the new date is booked immediately, not left as a vague promise.

### For fitness trainers and coaches

- Book sessions in blocks rather than one at a time, for example a four-week block of twice-weekly sessions.
- Record the session type: one-to-one, small group, online, gym-based, or outdoor.
- Keep a consistent slot for regular clients, and use the shareable booking page for new inquiries and trial sessions.
- Plan for missed sessions in advance: state whether a session can be rescheduled, how much notice is needed, and what happens if a client does not attend.
- Review the block at the end: what improved, what to change, and whether to renew for another block.

### A booking routine that works for both

1. Take the booking and confirm the date, time, location, and service.
2. Add it to the calendar immediately — do not rely on memory or a chat message.
3. Share the booking details with the client in writing.
4. Send a reminder before the appointment.
5. After the visit or session, update the client record and book or confirm the next appointment.

## 2. Client notes: small details that keep clients coming back

Client notes are short, useful facts that help you deliver a personal service without having to ask the same questions every time.

### What to record for cleaning clients

- Access details: how to enter, where to park, and any alarm or gate information the client has provided.
- Property notes: number of rooms, pets, surfaces that need special care, and areas to prioritize.
- Products and equipment: what the client provides, what you bring, and any products to avoid.
- Preferences: fragrance-free products, quiet hours, or rooms to leave untouched.
- History: last deep clean, recurring issues, and feedback after each visit.

### What to record for fitness clients

- Goals: what the client wants to achieve, in their own words, and by when.
- Health and safety notes: injuries, limitations, and anything a coach must know before a session. Only record what the client has shared for training purposes, and keep it private.
- Training history: exercises tried, progress, and what worked or did not work.
- Preferences: preferred training style, session length, location, and times of day.
- Communication notes: how the client likes to be contacted and how often they want check-ins.

### Rules for good client notes

- Keep notes short, factual, and current. Update them after a visit or session, not weeks later.
- Separate facts from opinions. Write "client prefers morning sessions" rather than a judgment about the client.
- Keep sensitive information private. Share only what the person doing the job needs to know.
- Review notes before the appointment, not during it.

## 3. Payment tracking without chasing your tail

Good payment tracking is not complicated. It means knowing, for every client, what was booked, what was invoiced, what was paid, and what is still owed.

### A simple payment workflow

1. Agree the price and payment terms before the work starts.
2. Create a branded invoice for the job, session block, or monthly service.
3. Send the invoice promptly — ideally on the same day as the work.
4. Record payment as soon as it arrives, with the date and method.
5. Follow up politely and promptly on overdue invoices, with the original invoice attached.
6. Review unpaid invoices weekly, not at the end of the month.

### For cleaning businesses

- Use repeat invoices for regular clients so monthly work is billed consistently.
- Track extras separately, such as a deep clean added to a standard visit, so the client can see exactly what they paid for.
- Keep a record of deposits or advance payments for larger jobs, such as end-of-tenancy cleaning.

### For fitness trainers and coaches

- Invoice by session block or monthly package, rather than chasing payment after every single session.
- Record package balances clearly: how many sessions were bought, how many were used, and how many remain.
- Set a clear policy for cancellations and unused sessions, and apply it consistently.

### Monthly finance check

- Total invoiced this month.
- Total received this month.
- Total still outstanding, and who owes it.
- Repeat clients versus one-off clients.
- Services or packages that bring in the most repeat work.

This monthly check turns booking and payment records into useful business information, without needing a separate accounting system for day-to-day tracking.

- **Monday:** regular office cleans, invoices sent the same day.
- **Tuesday to Thursday:** recurring home cleans, with client notes checked before each visit.

- **End of week:** confirm next week's recurring bookings and send reminders.

## Example week: fitness trainer

- **Monday and Wednesday:** regular one-to-one clients in fixed slots.
- **Tuesday:** small-group session, booked through the shareable booking page.
- **Thursday:** online check-ins and program updates for coaching clients.
- **Friday:** review session packages used and remaining, and invoice renewals for the next block.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Booking a recurring client only one session at a time, then losing the slot.
- Keeping client details only in chat messages that are hard to search later.
- Sending invoices late, then forgetting which ones are unpaid.
- Recording a payment in one place but not updating the client record.
- Changing a booking verbally without updating the calendar.
- Collecting intake information after the first visit, when it was needed before it.
